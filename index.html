    About the session

    Face and gesture recognition have become core interaction modalities in consumer VR/AR platforms, collectively known as extended reality (XR) environments in the form of photorealistic talking head-based avatar animation, hands-free authentication, social presence, and accessibility. Delivering these experiences requires continuous capture of high-resolution facial imagery, eye-gaze, hand-skeletal, and body-motion data streams.

    Recent studies show that these streams are independently sufficient to re-identify users with high confidence for reliable authentication. But at the same time, correlation between data streams (e.g., gaze and head motion) resists anonymization and is prone to security attacks by adversaries. Further, they can also leak sensitive inferences pertaining to age, health indicators, emotional and cognitive state that compromise user privacy. These risks present challenging problems for on-device capture, differential privacy for behavioral biometrics, liveness detection for gesture-based authentication, and cross-platform data governance.

    FG is the principal venue for face and gesture algorithmic research, yet the privacy and security implications specific to immersive capture remain underrepresented. This session extends FG's core expertise into this fast-growing application domain in XR. The proposed special session will foster collaboration between researchers, practitioners, policy stakeholders and industry experts to encourage novel methodologies, explore new applications, and identify challenges associated with improving trustworthiness in extended reality.

    Topics of interest

    Topics of interest include but are not limited to the following list.

    • Face, gaze, gesture, and behavior-based recognition and authentication in XR environments
    • Multimodal behavioral biometrics and cross-modal fusion of face, gaze, voice, hand, head, and body-motion signals
    • Liveness detection, presentation attack detection, and spoofing resilience for XR biometric systems
    • Deepfakes, synthetic avatars, avatar impersonation, and identity manipulation in immersive environments
    • Threat modeling and adversarial attacks for biometric capture in headsets, smart glasses, and other XR devices
    • Re-identification and de-anonymization from gaze, hand, head, and body-motion data
    • Robustness and scalable generalization across users, sessions, devices, and XR platforms
    • Deducing health cues from data in XR settings
    • Privacy-preserving, federated, differential-privacy, and signal-degradation approaches for XR biometrics
    • On-device and resource-efficient recognition, authentication, and privacy protection
    • Datasets, benchmarks, evaluation protocols, and bias analysis for XR biometrics and security
    • Regulatory and standards perspectives, including GDPR, BIPA, EU AI Act, and ISO/IEC
    • Usable privacy, consent, transparency, and user control in immersive interfaces

    Dr. Sudipta Banerjee

    Assistant Professor, Electrical Engineering and Computer Science
    University of Wyoming, USA

    Dr. Sudipta Banerjee is currently appointed as an Assistant Professor in the Department of Electrical Engineering and Computer Science at University of Wyoming, United States. Prior to that she was affiliated with New York University Tandon School of Engineering as a Research Assistant Professor in Computer Science and Engineering. She received her doctoral degree in Computer Science from Michigan State University. Her research focuses on biometrics, generative models and image forensics.

    She has served as an Area Chair in IEEE FG 2026 and Program Chair in the 11th International Conference on Computer Vision and Image Processing (CVIP 2026). Dr. Banerjee has organized the Special Session on Generative AI for Futuristic Biometrics at IEEE/IAPR International Joint Conference in Biometrics (IJCB) 2024. She has (co)-authored multiple research papers for peer-reviewed journals and presented her work at international conferences. She has received Best Paper Awards at IEEE International Conf. on Biometrics: Theory, Applications and Systems (BTAS) 2019, IAPR/IEEE-IWBF 2018 and IEEE-ISBA 2019.

    Dr. Marija Ivanovska

    Assistant, Faculty of Electrical Engineering
    University of Ljubljana, Slovenia

    Dr. Marija Ivanovska is an Assistant at the Faculty of Electrical Engineering, University of Ljubljana, Slovenia, and a member of the Laboratory for Machine Intelligence. Her research focuses on biometric security, computer vision, and trustworthy artificial intelligence, with particular emphasis on face-based attacks on biometric systems and the use of foundation models and multimodal large language models for robust biometric security.

    She has held international research appointments as a Visiting Scholar at Johns Hopkins University and as a Visiting Researcher at Queensland University of Technology, fostering collaborations in biometrics and trustworthy AI. She has authored numerous publications in peer-reviewed journals and conferences in computer vision, biometrics, and information forensics. Marija is actively involved in the international research community through editorial, organizational, and reviewing roles. She is a member of the IEEE Information Forensics and Security Technical Committee and the IAPR Technical Committee on Biometrics, and serves on the Webinar Committee of the IEEE Biometrics Council.
